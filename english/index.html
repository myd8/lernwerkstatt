Quantifiers

  • Countable: Many and a few: many bottles, a few apples.
  • Uncountable: Much and a little: much water, a little sugar.
  • Both: A lot of: a lot of bottles, a lot of water.
  • Some / any: Usually some in positive sentences and any in negatives or questions: “We have some milk.” “Do you have any milk?” Offers and requests often use some.

Indefinite pronouns

  • People / things: Someone, anyone, no one; something, anything, nothing.
  • Examples: “Someone called.” · “Did anyone call?” · “No one called.”

Talking about the future

  • Existing plan: Going to: “I’m going to visit my cousin this weekend.”
  • Decision made now: Will: “The phone’s ringing.” “I’ll get it.”
  • Arrangement: Present continuous: “I’m meeting Sam at three.”

Making predictions

  • Prediction: Will or won’t + base verb: “The river will freeze.”
  • How sure?: “It’ll probably freeze.” · “It probably won’t freeze.” · “It might freeze.”
  • Future continuous: Will be + -ing for an activity in progress at a future time: “At six we’ll be crossing the river.”

Cities of the future

  • Greener cities: Shaded pavements, bike lanes, district heating, rainwater collection.
  • Technology: Driverless shuttles, sensor-controlled lighting, raised walkways.
  • Comparison: “There might be fewer private cars but more shared buses.”

Sentence structure

  • Statements: Subject → verb → object: “Mia likes cycling.”
  • Questions: With most verbs in the present simple, use do or does: “Does Mia like cycling?” With be: “Is Mia ready?”

Daily routines and present tenses

  • Routine: Present simple: “I take the bus every day.”
  • Happening now: Present continuous: “I’m waiting for the bus.”
  • Frequency: Usually before the main verb, but after be: “I often walk.” “I’m often early.”

Storytelling and past tenses

  • Finished event: Past simple: “The lights went out.” Some past forms are irregular: go → went, see → saw.
  • Ongoing background: Past continuous: “We were watching a film when the lights went out.”
  • Linking words: At first, then, suddenly, after that, in the end.

Meeting and describing people

  • Appearance: “She has curly hair.” · “He wears glasses.”
  • Personality: “She’s friendly.” · “He’s quite shy at first.”
  • Conversation: “Nice to meet you.” · “What do you like doing?”

Reading a short story

  • Key terms: Setting, narrator, characters, conflict, turning point.
  • Text evidence: Point → detail from the text → what it suggests. Narrator ≠ author.

Food and seasonal traditions

  • Food vocabulary: Ingredients, recipe, menu, starter, main course, dessert.
  • Comparisons: Place + custom. Useful qualifiers: “some people”, “in parts of …”.

Spelling, numbers and time

  • Spelling: “How do you spell your name?”
  • Numbers: Thirteen (13) vs. thirty (30); three vs. third.
  • Time: 7:15 → “a quarter past seven”; 7:45 → “a quarter to eight.”
